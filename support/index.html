MoonlightJob support

MoonlightJob helps hourly workers decide whether a shift at a nearby game, concert or show is worth more than the shift they already have. It shows what each job would leave you per hour after travel time and your costs, and when nobody publishes the pay, what you'd need to make for it to be worth it.

Moonlight doesn't post jobs, hire anyone or take a cut. The employer (a venue, a caterer, a staffing app) decides who works.

What the app covers

  • The United States: the 50 states and DC. It's sold on the US App Store only.
  • iPhone, with iOS 18 or later.
  • Free, with no account, no sign-in, no purchases and no ads.
  • For adults, 18 and over.
  • Events: games, concerts and shows within your search radius (40 miles to start) in the next 7 days.
  • "Busy driving hours" appear in Chicago only, because it's the one US city that publishes its rideshare trips in a form the app can read.

Delete your data

Everything Moonlight knows about you is on your phone. To wipe it: Profile → What we do with your data → Delete everything. Moonlight's server keeps nothing tied to you, so there is nothing to ask us to delete there. A backup you exported is wherever you sent it.

Common questions

I don't see any events

If nothing is scheduled within your search radius this week, the Events tab says so. Raise it under Profile → Getting around → Search radius. If the app says "Events are unavailable right now", it couldn't reach its events server; pull down on the list to try again.

Why is there no pay for driving?

Rideshare companies don't publish what drivers earn, and Moonlight never guesses. It shows what you'd need to make per hour for the drive to be worth it. Log what you actually made after a night (Profile → Log a night you worked), and next time it uses your own numbers.

Where does "typical pay" come from?

From the US Bureau of Labor Statistics wage survey for your metro area or state, or the local minimum wage where that is higher. It's typical pay, marked as an estimate, not an offer: call the venue to find out what it pays.

I'm outside the US

Moonlight covers the 50 states and DC. With your location on outside them, it says so and looks nothing up. To look around a US city, turn Location off for Moonlight in the iPhone's Settings, then set a US city or ZIP under Profile.

I'd rather not share my location

You don't have to. Type a US city or ZIP during setup or under Profile → Where you are, and every screen works from that place. Location is used only while the app is open, never in the background.

The app says it's for adults and won't open

Where the law asks apps to check age, the iPhone asks whether to share your age range with Moonlight. The app opens once Apple confirms you're 18 or over. Tap "Try again" if you declined by mistake. Moonlight never stores your age.

Moonlight crashed

Open Profile → Diagnostics and share the diagnostics file with us by email. It holds the app and iOS versions, the iPhone model, the crash reports and the app's own log lines, never your location or what you earned.

I got a new phone

Before you switch, use Profile → Back up my data and keep the file. On the new phone, Profile → Restore from a backup. Your logged nights are the one thing Moonlight can't fetch again.

Where the numbers come from

Typical pay: US Bureau of Labor Statistics

Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025, published May 15, 2026, retrieved September 25, 2026. Hourly medians for ushers, concession workers, security guards, cleanup and parking attendants, by metro area or state.

BLS.gov cannot vouch for the data or analyses derived from these data after the data have been retrieved from BLS.gov.

Busy driving hours: City of Chicago

Transportation Network Providers trips, data.cityofchicago.org, averaged by hour by Moonlight.

This site provides applications using data that has been modified for use from its original source, www.cityofchicago.org, the official website of the City of Chicago. The City of Chicago makes no claims as to the content, accuracy, timeliness, or completeness of any of the data provided at this site. The data provided at this site is subject to change at any time. It is understood that the data provided at this site is being used at one’s own risk.

Gas prices: US Energy Information Administration

Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration (Weekly Retail Gasoline and Diesel Prices, 2026). The app names the week each price is from.

Which metro you're in: Census Bureau, BLS and USGS

US Census Bureau, Vintage 2024 metropolitan area population estimates (the July 2023 metro delineation) and 2024 Gazetteer files; the BLS list of wage-survey areas; and the US Geological Survey's Geographic Names Information System for each principal city's map point.

Weather: Apple Weather

Weather and hourly forecasts from Apple Weather. Other data sources. Moonlight uses the forecast to add a rain allowance to driving time, so that allowance is Moonlight's use of Apple's data, not Apple's figure.

Events: Ticketmaster

Games, concerts and shows from the Ticketmaster Discovery API, fetched by Moonlight's own server. Where a league publishes an average crowd, the app shows it and names the league; otherwise it says "size n/a".

Maps, venues and travel times: Apple Maps

Where venues are, their phone numbers and websites, drive and transit times, and the next ride home by transit.

Everything else the app cites on screen

  • Minimum wages: each city, county or state's own page, notice or poster, read on the event's date.
  • Transit fares: each agency's own fares page.
  • Miles per gallon, and the rain allowance's range: Federal Highway Administration.
  • Car upkeep per mile: AAA, Your Driving Costs 2026.
  • How much of a rideshare driver's time has a rider: UC Berkeley Labor Center, Chicago study, January 2025.
